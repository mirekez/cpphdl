WHY THE NGSPICE ROWS RANGE FROM MINUTES TO HOURS

These are whole test wall times, not measured ngspice CPU times. In particular,
row 5 really does use ngspice; its short duration is not a digital-only shortcut.

Row 5 (5m 45s): One ADC slice and one conversion, with both schematic and
extracted-sampler simulations. The retained ngspice logs report 65,148 and
64,865 output rows. The deck simulates startup to 16 us and records the final
100 ns conversion window, stopping at 16.1 us. Recorded samples therefore do
not describe all internal integration work during startup.

Rows 6 and 7 (18h 13m 29s and 17h 38m 6s): Different transmitter circuits and
component-RC candidates, not repeats of the ADC fixture. Their physical runs
report 216,236 and 216,117 output rows. Row 7 covers 1.544 us. Its shorter
simulated interval does not imply less work per step or less total wall time.
The retained logs do not separate queueing, solver computation and checking,
or report enough convergence/matrix statistics to establish why row 7 took
about 184 times the wall time of row 5. Sample count alone does not explain it.

Row 9 (25h 53m 12s): A 64 us closed-loop oscillator/gate transient, with
13,687,377 output rows. Its CTest log explicitly records waiting for the shared
EDA memory slot. That wait is included in the table; its duration was not
separately recorded.

In general, nonlinear transistor simulation cost depends on circuit equations,
device models, adaptive internal steps, convergence iterations and rejected
steps, in addition to simulated duration and waveform I/O. Output rows are not
solver-iteration counts. These are possible runtime factors, not measured
attributions of the hours in this audit. See the ngspice manual:
https://ngspice.sourceforge.io/docs/ngspice-manual.pdf

A reliable performance comparison needs separate queue/build/solver/checker
wall times, solver CPU time, circuit size and integration/convergence counters.
This historical table does not contain that profiling and cannot establish a
100x C++RTL speedup or equivalent simulation accuracy.

WHY IT HAS TAKEN SO LONG

Long transistor-level simulations and repeated physical timing failures dominate
many retained runs. Three long clock/transmitter tests recorded 61 h 45 m of
combined elapsed test time, including waiting/checking where applicable.
Framework, exporter, circuit and physical-flow development proceeded together.
Avoidable rework included late interface checks, incomplete route updates, and
a gate-model build that ran despite failed extracted timing. These workflow
problems have required additional fixes and regression tests.
